title: "What Is the Process of a Wastewater Treatment Plant?"

---

A wastewater treatment plant exists to remove contaminants from sewage or industrial wastewater so that the resulting effluent can be safely discharged to the environment or reused, protecting both public health and the receiving water body.

## Preliminary and primary treatment

Incoming wastewater first passes through screens and grit removal to strip out rags and heavy inorganic solids, then into a primary clarifier where suspended organic solids settle out as primary sludge.

## Secondary treatment

This is the biological heart of the plant. Bacteria are given air and time, typically in an aeration tank, to consume dissolved and suspended organic matter, after which a second clarifier separates the resulting biological floc from the treated water.

## Tertiary treatment and disinfection

Where the discharge standard requires it, further filtration or membrane treatment removes remaining nutrients and fine solids, and a final disinfection step, chlorination, UV or ozone, inactivates remaining pathogens.

## Sludge handling

The sludge collected during primary and secondary treatment is separately thickened and dewatered, and is either disposed of or, in some cases, reused as compost or agricultural fertiliser, depending on local regulation.
