date: 2022-11-25

---

Getting more consistent, higher-quality effluent out of an existing wastewater treatment plant usually comes down to strengthening the same three stages every plant already has, rather than adding entirely new technology.

## Primary treatment

The goal of primary treatment is to obtain water free of debris and undesirable material before biological treatment begins. This relies on screens to catch rags and solids, a grit chamber to settle out sand, cinders and stones, and a sedimentation tank to remove finer suspended matter. Plants that skimp on primary treatment tend to see fouling and reduced efficiency further downstream, so this stage is worth getting right first.

## Secondary treatment

Secondary treatment is typically carried out either through a trickling filter or an activated sludge process. In the activated sludge approach, air is introduced into an aeration tank alongside sludge-forming bacteria, which break down the dissolved and suspended organic matter that survives primary treatment. Maintaining the right balance of air, retention time and bacterial population is usually the single biggest lever for improving overall treatment performance.

## Final disinfection

Before the treated water is discharged, chlorination (or an equivalent UV/ozone step) removes remaining pathogens, this final step is what protects public health and the receiving water body.

In practice, most efficiency gains come from right-sizing the aeration stage to the actual load the plant receives, and from choosing a treatment technology, EA, SAFF, SBR, MBBR or MBR, that matches the site's space and discharge requirements.
