---

Excess iron in groundwater is one of the most common water quality complaints in areas that rely on borewells, it shows up as a metallic taste, reddish-brown staining on fixtures and laundry, and an unpleasant odour. An iron removal plant addresses this through a straightforward oxidation-and-filtration process.

## 1. Aeration

Raw water is aerated to introduce oxygen, beginning the conversion of dissolved ferrous iron (Fe2+) into its insoluble ferric form (Fe3+).

## 2. Catalytic oxidation

The water passes through a bed of catalytic media that accelerates this oxidation reaction well beyond what aeration alone achieves.

## 3. Precipitation and filtration

Once converted, the now-insoluble iron precipitates out and is captured in the filter bed as the water passes through, leaving clear, iron-free water downstream.

## 4. Backwash regeneration

Periodically reversing the flow through the media bed flushes out the accumulated iron precipitate and restores the filter's capacity, this is what gives the media its multi-year working life.
