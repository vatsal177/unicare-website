---
title: "How Is Water Purified in a Water Treatment Plant?"
date: 2023-02-20
excerpt: "A walkthrough of the stages raw water passes through before it reaches the tap: intake, filtration, disinfection and distribution."
---

Municipal and industrial water treatment plants exist to turn raw water, drawn from a river, lake, reservoir or borewell, into water that is safe and pleasant to use. While the exact configuration varies by source and end use, most plants move water through the same broad sequence of stages.

## 1. Intake and screening

Raw water is drawn in through an intake point and passed through coarse screens that remove leaves, debris and larger suspended matter before it enters the treatment line.

## 2. Coagulation and sedimentation

Chemical coagulants are dosed into the water to bind fine suspended particles into larger clumps (floc), which then settle out in a sedimentation tank under gravity.

## 3. Filtration

The clarified water passes through sand, gravel or multi-media filters to remove any remaining fine particles, followed by activated carbon filtration where taste, odour or organic compounds need to be addressed.

## 4. Disinfection

Chlorine, ozone or UV treatment is applied to inactivate bacteria, viruses and other pathogens that survive the filtration stage, this is the step that makes the water microbiologically safe.

## 5. Storage and distribution

Treated water is held in clean storage tanks before being pumped into the distribution network, ready for use.

Each stage exists to solve a specific problem, physical particles, dissolved organics, or biological contamination, so the right combination of stages depends on what's actually in your raw water.
