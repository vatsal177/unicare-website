---

Wastewater treatment is a multi-stage process that removes contaminants from domestic or industrial wastewater before it is discharged or reused. Below is the typical eight-step sequence.

## 1. Screening

Removes large solids, rags, plastics and debris that could damage downstream equipment.

## 2. Grit removal

Sand, grit and other heavy inorganic particles are settled out to protect pumps and pipework from abrasion.

## 3. Primary sedimentation

Wastewater is held in a clarifier tank, allowing organic solids to settle out as primary sludge.

## 4. Aeration / biological treatment

Air is introduced to encourage aerobic bacteria to break down dissolved and suspended organic matter, this is the core of secondary treatment.

## 5. Secondary sedimentation

The treated water passes through a second clarifier, separating out the biological floc produced during aeration.

## 6. Tertiary / advanced treatment

Where a higher effluent standard is required, additional filtration or membrane treatment removes remaining suspended solids and nutrients.

## 7. Disinfection

Chlorination, UV or ozone treatment inactivates remaining pathogens before discharge.

## 8. Sludge handling

Sludge collected from the primary and secondary stages is thickened, dewatered and disposed of or reused, for example as compost, depending on local regulation.
