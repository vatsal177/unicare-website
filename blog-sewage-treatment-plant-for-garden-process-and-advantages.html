Sewage Treatment Plant for Garden: Process & Advantages

How onsite packaged STPs let housing societies and parks reuse treated sewage for irrigation, illustrated with two real Pune installations.

Reusing treated sewage for garden and landscape irrigation is one of the more practical ways housing societies, parks and campuses can cut their fresh water and water-tanker costs. Unicare's packaged Sewage Treatment Plants (STPs) are built specifically to support this kind of onsite reuse.

How it works

Wastewater is drawn from a nearby open drain (nala) by pumping and collected in a collection tank. From there it's transferred to the STP for treatment. The treated outlet water is collected in a filter feed tank, then polished further through a pressure sand filter (PSF) and activated carbon filter (ACF), with disinfection dosing applied before the water is released for irrigation.

Available capacities

These packaged STP units are available from 1 KLD up to 300 KLD, and can be installed either above ground or underground depending on site conditions and available space.

Real installations

Unicare has installed a 5 KLD garden STP at Premlok Park, Pune, and a 10 KLD STP at Oxygen Park, Pune. The 5 KLD Premlok Park installation alone saves an estimated 18 lakh litres of fresh water per year that would otherwise be drawn from municipal supply or tankers.

Why it matters

For housing societies and public gardens facing rising water tanker costs or municipal supply restrictions, a garden STP converts an ongoing waste stream into a reliable, low-cost irrigation source.
